# ADR 0004: Recover abandoned provisioning claims from a detached worker

Status: Accepted for issue #58. Implemented in PR #60. Decisions 5 to 7 and 9 to 10 were added or revised when that PR was reviewed.

## Context

A provisioning step (worktree create, agent start, brief) records a durable claim: a step claim (`step_claim_*`) and, for a queued task or a task's first step, a slot claim (`slot_*`). Claims are lease-based, and nothing ran when a lease expired. A claim whose process had died was recovered only when some unrelated coordinator operation ran after the lease, so the task could stay parked indefinitely (issue #58). A restart inside the lease, or a quiet project, parked it.

The issue listed three options: take over at once when the owner is gone, run a delayed recovery process after the lease, or add a deliberate retry. This record chooses the second, with the owner identity from the first.

## Decision

1. **Claims record the owner's process identity.** Slot and step claims store the PID and creation time of the process that took them (`state.ProcessIdentity`). A claim whose owner is alive is never taken over. A claim whose owner cannot be checked is kept, not taken over. Legacy rows without an identity keep the lease-only rule.
2. **A detached worker resumes what the lease released.** Startup, the bridge's `dispatch`, `review`, `ticket_move` and `adjudicate_brief`, and board ticket moves arm a detached recovery worker before the route runs (`startRecoveryWorker`). A route that cannot arm its worker is refused before it does anything. The worker waits for the route's completion signal or for its parent process to exit. It then fences the route's own claims (`CompleteRecoveryClaims`) and runs `RecoverUntilIdle` over that scope: the claims of the completed operation, or every claim in the store for the startup scope.
3. **Dead owners wait for their lease.** The worker sleeps until a claim reaches the lease deadline recorded on it, then takes it over: the recorded deadline decides, not the time the owner died. Live owners are polled once a second and never taken over. Taking over at once was not chosen. The store's takeover gates (`ClaimStep`, `SlotClaimAbandoned`) are lease-based, and nothing in the store shows that a dead process has no Herdr call still running. [INFERENCE: the lease is what bounds that window; no code comment states it.]
4. **Recovery continues only a step that Herdr proves.** A recorded step is continued only for a pane that proves it is the task's worker, or for an empty pane of the task's own checkout (`reconcileTask`). Otherwise the step is declined: nothing is started or submitted, and the reason is recorded as the task's attention (`recoveryDeclined`), so a person sees it on the board.
   - A declined claim is **not** excluded from recovery. Attention is also set by failures whose claims must still be recovered, such as a transient agent-start refusal. Excluding attention would park exactly the claims this decision exists to resume.
5. **Each claim is paced on its own, and recovery terminates.** A claim a pass declined waits its own backoff before the next attempt: 1s, 2s, 4s, 8s, 16s, then 30s between seven declines (`defaultRecoveryPace`). A claim that has used its seven declines is exhausted for this worker. The worker stops with `ErrRecoveryStalled` once no claim is left that it can attempt or await. The stop reports the most declines any remaining claim took. Those claims stay recorded and keep their slots until startup, or a drain of a queued task, continues them. Waiting for a live owner is not a stall.
6. **One refusal does not starve its scope.** Each due claim is judged on its own and failures are collected. A refused claim waits its own backoff, so a later claim in the same scope is still resumed when it comes due.
7. **Background passes judge only due claims.** A pass judges only the claims `Store.RecoveryClaims` reports: due, held by no live or unverifiable owner, in the `intent`, `worktree_created` or `agent_started` phase, and neither blocked nor dispositioned. A running worker with no claim, a task whose claim a live process holds, and a cleanup are never judged here. Only startup's full `Reconcile` settles in-flight inbox deliveries (`RecoverInbox`), settles interrupted cleanups and flushes queued notes. A background pass can run while a live route is delivering or removing a checkout, so it does none of these.
8. **Liveness is the process handle's state.** `ProcessAlive` opens the process with `SYNCHRONIZE` and asks for a zero-timeout wait on the handle. It does not read the exit code, because `GetExitCodeProcess` reports 259 for a process that exited with that code too.
9. **A worker never waits on a claim its own pass left.** A pass returns only after its steps end, so a claim this process still holds for an attempted task is abandoned. A refused step keeps the slot it renewed under the worker's identity; a definite refusal also releases the step claim, while an undecided one keeps it. A live owner is never taken over, so waiting on such a claim would never end. After each attempt the worker fences the attempted tasks for its own identity (`MarkTaskComplete`), which makes their claims due at the lease end. Any later acquisition by this process retires that fence (`markTaskActive`), so the fence is applied again after every attempt. `TestRecoveryDoesNotWaitOnAClaimItsOwnPassLeft` waits until its deadline when the fence is removed.
10. **Recovery admits no work over a held slot.** A resumed step keeps the capacity it holds, and a refused worker start or brief keeps its slot too, so queued work waits for the next trigger (a review, a dispatch or startup). Recovery releases only the capacity its own attempt took, and it admits a queued intent only for that claim's task (`drainQueuedTask`), through the same capacity accounting a dispatch uses. Among provisioning paths, a slot is released only when a definite, non-terminal worktree-create refusal is recorded (the queue then drains through the same gates a dispatch passes), a retry cannot clear its own attention, a dispatch's dependency recheck fails or finds the task gated after it claimed the slot, or a caller gives back a claim it brought without owning the step. A recorded review disposition is a separate lifecycle path: it takes its task out of the slot count (`slotHolder`).
11. **Out of scope.** Bind intents keep their separate explicit proof path (AGENTS.md). A deliberate retry of a parked task by the primary or the board (the issue's third scenario) is not part of this decision.

## Consequences

- A claim left by a route is resumed by that route's own worker: once the route completes or its parent exits, the worker fences the route's claims and judges each one at its recorded lease deadline. Startup resumes any claim left in the store, and a queued task's claim is also continued by a later drain. The regression tests cover the stalled and refused cases, the background passes that must not settle or judge, the worker's own claims (decision 9), and the existing tests cover a queued planner, an ordinary queued task, and a later provisioning phase after the lease.
- The worker is not a service. It exits when no claim is left to attempt or await, when its route aborts it, or when every remaining claim is exhausted.
- **Residual: a live owner keeps its worker.** The worker polls a claim that a live process holds once a second, and does not stop while that process holds it. A process that stays open with a refused claim (for example a board left running) keeps the worker alive for as long as it holds the claim. [INFERENCE: read from the code; no test covers it.]
- A decline, a checkout problem and a refused start or brief are recorded as the task's attention; a terminal refusal records a blocker instead. A failure to read Herdr or the store leaves no record beyond the stored claim, because the worker's stderr is discarded (`io.Discard`).
- An exhausted claim keeps its slot, and `claimedSlots` counts it against the project's concurrency until startup or a queued-task drain resumes it. Decision 11 leaves a deliberate retry out of scope, so nothing in this decision releases it sooner.
- **Residual: Windows job objects.** The worker is configured to survive Herdr's hook runner (`CREATE_BREAKAWAY_FROM_JOB | DETACHED_PROCESS`). That was not verified in Herdr's job-object environment here; the acceptance suite is the check. A route whose worker cannot arm refuses, so this fails closed.
- Considered and rejected:
  - Taking over at once on owner death: needs proof that no call from the dead process is still running, which the store cannot give.
  - Excluding attention-marked tasks from recovery: parks transient failures (decision 4).
  - Setting a blocker on a declined claim: a blocker is a human-only decision and refuses `ResumeDispatch`, so it would turn a transient refusal into a manual step.
  - A whole-project background reconcile: it settles cleanups and delivers inbox notes while a live route may still be doing so (decision 7).
